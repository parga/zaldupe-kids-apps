# Most commonly used apps between kids 7-14


## Common risks  

- **Cyberbullying**: Bullying that takes place over digital devices like phones, computers, or tablets, often through social media, messaging, or gaming platforms.
- **Body image issues**: Negative perceptions or feelings about one's own body, often influenced by unrealistic standards seen on social media or through peer comparison.
- **Grooming**: When an adult builds a relationship, trust, and emotional connection with a child online to manipulate, exploit, or abuse them, often for sexual purposes.
- **Sexual harassment**: Unwanted or inappropriate sexual comments, messages, or advances, often occurring through chat, messaging, or social media platforms.
- **Predatory contact**: When adults or older individuals attempt to contact and exploit children online, often for abusive or illegal purposes.
- **Addiction**: Excessive or compulsive use of apps or games that interferes with daily life, school, or relationships.
- **Privacy breaches**: Unauthorized access, sharing, or exposure of personal information, such as location, contacts, or private messages.
- **Scams**: Fraudulent schemes designed to trick users into giving away money, personal information, or account access.

### Apps that our kids are exposed to

### Youtube

- Description: Video sharing and streaming platform for entertainment, education, and user-generated content.
- Original intent: Allow users to upload, share, and view videos globally.
- Privacy: Collects viewing history, search data, device info, and may track location; public comments and profiles.
- Common misuse: Watching inappropriate content, oversharing in comments, exposure to ads and influencer marketing.
- Danger areas: Exposure to inappropriate content, cyberbullying in comments, addiction, privacy breaches.
- Risk level: High

### Youtube kids

- Description: Child-friendly version of YouTube with curated content and parental controls.
- Original intent: Provide a safer video platform for children with age-appropriate content.
- Privacy: Collects viewing history and device info; parental controls available but not foolproof.
- Common misuse: Accessing content not suitable for age, excessive screen time.
- Danger areas: Exposure to inappropriate or misleading content, addiction.
- Risk level: Mid

### Tik Tok
- Description: Short-form video sharing app focused on music, dance, and creative expression.
- Original intent: Enable users to create and share entertaining short videos.
- Privacy: Collects extensive personal data; public profiles by default; location, contacts, and device info often shared.
- Common misuse: Sharing personal info, exposure to inappropriate content, risky challenges.
- Danger areas: Cyberbullying, sexual harassment, predatory contact, addiction.
- Risk level: High

### Snapchat 
- Description: Multimedia messaging app with disappearing messages, photos, and stories.
- Original intent: Facilitate quick, ephemeral communication and sharing among friends.
- Privacy: Messages/photos disappear, but can be saved; location sharing (Snap Map); collects contacts and usage data.
- Common misuse: Sexting, sharing private info, bullying via disappearing messages.
- Danger areas: Sexual harassment, cyberbullying, privacy breaches.
- Risk level: High

### Instagram
- Description: Photo and video sharing social network with stories and messaging.
- Original intent: Allow users to share visual content and connect with others.
- Privacy: Public profiles by default; collects location, contacts, and behavioral data.
- Common misuse: Oversharing, exposure to influencers, cyberbullying, fake accounts.
- Danger areas: Bullying, sexual harassment, body image issues, predatory contact.
- Risk level: High

### Discord
- Description: Voice, video, and text chat platform for communities and gaming groups.
- Original intent: Provide gamers with a communication tool for group chats and coordination.
- Privacy: Chat servers can be public/private; collects usage data; voice/video chat.
- Common misuse: Joining unsafe servers, exposure to adult content, sharing personal info.
- Danger areas: Grooming, bullying, sexual harassment, exposure to explicit content.
- Risk level: High

### Videogames

#### Roblox
- Privacy: In-game chat, public profiles; collects usage and behavioral data.
- Common misuse: Chatting with strangers, sharing personal info, exposure to inappropriate games.
- Danger areas: Grooming, bullying, scams, sexual content in user-generated games.
- Risk level: High

#### Minecraft 
- Privacy: Online multiplayer exposes kids to strangers; collects usage data.
- Common misuse: Chatting with strangers, bullying in-game, sharing personal info.
- Danger areas: Bullying, exposure to inappropriate language/content, predatory contact.
- Risk level: Mid

## Psychological or emotional changes

- **Anxiety**: Increased worry about social status, online interactions, or missing out (FOMO).
- **Depression**: Feelings of sadness or hopelessness due to cyberbullying, social comparison, or isolation.
- **Low self-esteem**: Negative self-image from unrealistic standards, peer pressure, or online criticism.
- **Sleep disturbances**: Poor sleep quality from late-night usage or screen exposure.
- **Social withdrawal**: Reduced real-life interactions, preferring online engagement over face-to-face relationships.
- **Impaired attention**: Difficulty focusing on schoolwork or tasks due to constant notifications and distractions.

## Addictive behaviors

Common addictions generated by the use of these apps/platforms include:

- **Social media addiction**: Compulsive checking of notifications, likes, comments, and constant scrolling (TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat, Discord).
- **Content consumption addiction**: Excessive watching of videos, streams, or stories, leading to loss of time and neglect of responsibilities (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube).
- **Gaming addiction**: Prolonged gaming sessions, prioritizing games over school, sleep, or social activities (Roblox, Minecraft).
- **Online social interaction addiction**: Excessive chatting, joining groups/servers, and seeking online validation or friendships (Discord, Roblox, Snapchat).


## Engagement and Retention
Common techniques used by these apps to drive engagement :

- **Notifications**: Alerts for likes, comments, messages, and updates to encourage frequent engagement.
- **Infinite scroll/feed**: Endless content streams to keep users browsing longer.
- **Likes, reactions, and rewards**: Social validation mechanisms that trigger dopamine responses.
- **Personalized recommendations**: Algorithms suggest content based on user behavior to increase relevance and time spent.
- **Streaks and achievements**: Features like Snapchat streaks or gaming achievements encourage daily use.
- **Social connectivity**: Group chats, friend lists, and sharing features foster ongoing interaction.
- **Live content**: Live streams and real-time events create urgency and FOMO (fear of missing out).


**Purpose of retention:**  
The primary purpose of user retention in these apps is to maximize engagement and keep users returning frequently. By retaining users, platforms can:

- **Increase time spent on the app**, which boosts opportunities for displaying ads and generating revenue.
- **Collect more user data** to improve targeted advertising, personalize recommendations, and refine algorithms.
- **Strengthen network effects**, where the value of the platform grows as more users join and interact, making it harder for users to leave.
- **Encourage habitual use**, making the app a regular part of users’ daily routines.
- **Drive in-app purchases or subscriptions** in games and social platforms.
- **Enhance brand loyalty**, ensuring users choose their platform over competitors.

Ultimately, retention strategies are designed to grow the platform’s user base, maximize profits, and maintain a dominant position in the market.
